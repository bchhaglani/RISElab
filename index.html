RISE Lab

Responsible & Intelligent Sensing for hEalth

Penn State University · College of Information Sciences and Technology

We explore how everyday devices can help us better understand and improve our health. Much of what shapes our health happens between clinical visits—in our daily activities, habits, and environments. We leverage ambient signals around us to help fill these gaps and provide meaningful insights into our holistic health, while protecting privacy and supporting human agency.

Our research combines ubiquitous computing, signal processing, machine learning, and human-centered computing across three connected directions:

  • Responsible sensing and AI: understanding what sensitive information sensors reveal, designing privacy-aware sensing, domain-adaptive protection, balancing privacy-utility-cost tradeoffs, and advancing trustworthy AI, explainability, and evaluation.
  • Mobile and wearable sensing for health: assessing voice health, enhancing speech accessibility, monitoring posture and everyday behaviors, and providing adaptive human-centered interventions.
  • Healthy environments: monitoring environmental exposure, indoor air parameters, mapping environments, and predicting their health risks.

We are building our team! We welcome interest from PhD, master’s, and undergraduate students.

01

Novel sensing for environment health

We develop ubiquitous sensing methods to monitor environmental exposure, assess indoor air, and map the environments around us. By combining sensor measurements with machine learning and knowledge of physical systems, we aim to predict their health risks. Moving forward, we are collaborating with architectural engineering experts to monitor children’s exposure in daycare environments.

Mobile and Wearable Sensing for Health

We use acoustic and wearable signals to understand health beyond clinical settings. Moving forward, we are collaborating with speech pathology experts to identify new biomarkers and improve communication for people with speech disorders.

03

Responsible Sensing and AI

How can sensing systems provide useful insights while respecting privacy and agency? We investigate unintended information leakage from audio and other sensor representations, develop privacy-aware methods with domain-adaptive protection, and advance trustworthy AI through explainability and evaluation. Moving forward, we are developing foundational privacy techniques, evaluation methodologies, and trustworthy health AI models.

People

Bhawana Chhaglani

Principal Investigator · Assistant Professor · Penn State IST

bbc5655@psu.edu
Website·Google Scholar

Bhawana Chhaglani is an Assistant Professor in the College of Information Sciences and Technology at The Pennsylvania State University. She received her Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of Massachusetts Amherst. Her research lies at the intersection of mobile and ubiquitous sensing, human-centered computing, and responsible AI for health and wellness. She designs intelligent sensing systems that leverage everyday devices to generate meaningful health insights while preserving user privacy and agency.

She has conducted research at Meta Reality Labs, Dolby Laboratories, Samsung Research, Nokia Bell Labs, and Microsoft Research. Her research has been recognized through the 2024 MLCommons Rising Star and 2025 ACM MobiSys Rising Star honors. Her doctoral research on privacy-aware audio sensing for healthier indoor environments received the Best Ph.D. Forum Award at ACM CPS-IoT Week 2025, and she was awarded a UMass Amherst Dissertation Fellowship.
